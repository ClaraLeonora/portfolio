About

I build adaptive learning tools that evaluate how students reason through complex problems, grounding automated feedback in cognitive misconception models rather than surface correctness. My research spans AI in education, learning analytics, and accessible computing outreach.

My background covers system design, evaluation, and community deployment. As a STARS AI Scholar, I co-designed K–12 AI-literacy curricula, facilitated computing activities for students with disabilities at the Reach for the Stars! fair, and co-authored research on scaling AI education to under-resourced schools. On the engineering side, I developed DNF-based verification algorithms, prototyped an adaptive mobile tutor, Proof-42, and co-authored work evaluating the pedagogical validity of LLM-generated logic hints.

I hold a B.S. in Mathematics and am completing a second B.S. in Computer Science with a minor in Business, a perspective shaped by TAing introductory Java and engineering backend services at NASA JPL. I approach educational technology as a systems problem: one that requires mathematical precision, pedagogical intent, and accessible design.
