About

I build adaptive learning tools and study whether automated feedback is actually correct and usable for the students who receive it. My research spans AI in education, intelligent tutoring systems, and accessible computing.

My background covers system design, evaluation, and community deployment. As a STARS AI Scholar, I co-designed K–12 AI-literacy activities, facilitated computing activities for students with disabilities at the Reach for the Stars! fair, and co-authored research on scaling AI literacy through K–12 outreach. On the engineering side, I developed a bidirectional prompting technique and a DNF-based proof solver for evaluating LLM-generated logic proofs, prototyped an adaptive mobile tutor, Proof-42, and co-authored work on the promise and limits of LLM-generated proofs and hints.

I hold a B.S. in Mathematics and am completing a second B.S. in Computer Science with a minor in Business, a perspective shaped by TAing introductory Java and engineering backend services at NASA JPL. I approach educational technology as a systems problem: one that requires mathematical precision, pedagogical intent, and accessible design.
